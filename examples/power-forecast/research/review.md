# Evidence review

**Mode:** author self-check using a second implementation, not a blinded review. No model was trained. The archive was obtained from the official versioned CSV; its length and SHA-256 matched `datapackage.json`. The digest of the primary results was unchanged after the exploratory run. The pre-result plan digest detects edits, not the date of an external preregistration.

**Calendar and denominator.** The production calculation enumerated physical UTC hours and matched Berlin wall times. For an independent arithmetic check, I read the verified CSV with `csv.DictReader` and SQLite, taking wall times directly from the source's `cet_cest_timestamp` (discarding its numeric offset), rather than deriving them with `zoneinfo`. I removed clock keys that occurred twice, then used a local-time self-join on `datetime(current.wall, '-7 days')`. I required actual, forecast and lagged actual to be present. The independent join recovered 8756 target hours. The main flow record shows four DST-related omissions (two target repeats, one missing lag clock time, one repeated lag clock time) and no missing-value omissions. The synthetic clock-change pilot also confirmed that two equal local hours a week apart can be 167 physical hours apart.

**Arithmetic.** SQLite `ORDER BY ... LIMIT 1 OFFSET ((9*n-1)/10)` (integer offset) gave 69058 MW for actuals and 67818 MW for the forecast midpoint. I calculated `avg(abs(f-y))`, `avg(abs(w-y))`, and separately `avg(abs(w-y)-abs(f-y))` on the self-joined rows. Unrounded weekly-minus-day-ahead differences were 539.9764732754683 MW overall, −159.4052511415525 MW for the realized-high group, 617.725 MW for its complement, −160.87899543378995 MW for the midpoint-high group and 617.8888324873096 MW for its complement. Both cutoffs selected 876 hours and their intersection contained 670. Counts agree exactly and all means/contrasts agree within the frozen tolerance of 0.000001 MW. The SVG labels and counts agree with the primary CSV after rounding; its bars start at zero.

**Source checks.** The original OPSD hourly schema explicitly describes both national columns and their MW units. Crossref metadata resolved DOI 10.3390/math12010019 to Tzortzis and colleagues' paper; the publisher full-text XML (not a search snippet) confirms the look-back benchmark in §2.5. The paper uses a 168-value look-back; this study instead matches civil-clock hours across DST. The citation supports the baseline choice, not our German error estimates. Excerpt locators and checksums are in [sources.json](sources.json).

**Conclusions.** C1–C2 and C5–C6 describe the finite-year arithmetic, C3–C4 the source definitions and prior benchmark, and C7–C8 the outcome of changing the subgroup definition on those same observations. The result does not imply that the weekly value was closer on *every* high-demand hour, or that either method would win on future peaks. The archive does not verify the issue-time information set; the cutoff is retrospective even in the forecast-midpoint sensitivity. Cause, performance outside 2019 and a live forecasting rule remain untested. No unresolved mismatch was found between the reported results and these inputs.
