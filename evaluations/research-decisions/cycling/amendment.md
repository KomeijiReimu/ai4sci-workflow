# Which summer’s counts can reconstruct a Williamsburg Bridge day?

**Recommend a paired test on recorded passages.** Use summer 2018 and summer 2019 separately to reconstruct the same dry Tuesday–Thursday counts from July 8 through August 31 in 2014 and 2015. This tests the assumption behind [NYC DOT’s conversion of historical daytime counts](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/east-river-bridge-counts-24hrs-1980-2025.pdf) (note 4), not the unobserved totals before 2014. [Roll’s Oregon study](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/open_access_etds/998/) (§3.5.1, Table 3.17) already tested summer peak-hour factors against independent full-day fall counts. The question here is whether changing the calibration year matters when the bridge, hours and test dates stay fixed.

**Accuracy and sensitivity answer different questions.** For each calibration year and each of six month-by-weekday groups, divide total 24-hour passages by total passages from 07:00 up to 19:00. Apply both factors to each eligible earlier day’s daytime count. Fix the estimates before revealing the omitted hours. For each test summer, let \(A\) be the recorded total on those same dates. Report each signed error, \(E_{18}=\hat A_{18}-A\) and \(E_{19}=\hat A_{19}-A\), in passages and as a percentage of \(A\). Their paired difference, \(E_{19}-E_{18}=\hat A_{19}-\hat A_{18}\), measures the effect of choosing a calibration year. A positive difference can mean **less undercounting**, not overcounting. As the [independent review](../review.md#cycling-bridge-opening--count-reconstruction) points out, a higher omitted-to-daytime ratio can also result from fewer daytime passages. Report mean passages per eligible day separately for daytime, 20:00–24:00 plus 00:00–05:00, and the remaining hours within each group.

**Coverage before inference.** The review found only 3–5 candidate dates per group per summer before exclusions, all with raw record status. Require every group in all four summers to retain at least three dry days from three distinct weeks, each with 96 unique, usable quarter-hours under the [documented eligibility rules](evidence.md#data-and-access); require positive calibration denominators. Otherwise stop the six-group comparison. Check the bin labels against the local clock; if a one-bin shift changes the decision, leave it unresolved. Drop one week at a time, refitting and comparing on paired dates, to show dependence on individual weeks. This is a sensitivity range, not a confidence interval. A resample with an empty group has no estimate: do not enter zero or discard such draws to manufacture an interval.

**Decision.** If both vintages stay within the proposed ±5% level-error tolerance in both test summers under week deletion, retain a simple conversion for comparable summer averages. If the choice robustly crosses that tolerance, show both estimates at the historical series join. If both miss, flag the conversion itself; if the conclusion depends on a week, report insufficient support. None of these outcomes establishes 5% accuracy for the cycling population, a shift from subway to bicycle, or trips induced by a bridge redesign.
