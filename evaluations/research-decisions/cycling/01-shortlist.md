# Which New York cycling question first?

**25 November 2026 · initial decision, retained before the challenge pass**

1. **Infrastructure decision — provisional first choice.** Did opening the protected roadway path on the Brooklyn Bridge in September 2021 bring additional bicycle trips across the East River or mainly shift them from the nearby Manhattan Bridge? An evaluator deciding what benefit to attribute to the conversion should care about the difference. [NYC DOT's November 2021 release](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2021/brooklyn-bridge-bike-ridership-skyrockets.shtml), the October comparison and following paragraph, reports a rise at the treated bridge and describes the other bridges as consistent with historical trends. [Kuchkovska, Lysetska & Fundak (2025)](https://doi.org/10.25140/2411-5363-2025-3%2841%29-310-321), Table 2, already compare Brooklyn against Manhattan. A new test would put both bridges in the outcome: a gain at Brooklyn with an offset at Manhattan predicts little change in the pair; additional cross-river riding predicts a gain in the pair relative to background traffic. The concern is whether any background series really supplies that comparison.

2. **Measurement decision — strongest alternative.** How far backward can a daytime count stand in for a full day? NYC DOT's [East River bridge series](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/east-river-bridge-counts-24hrs-1980-2025.pdf), notes 1, 4 and 5, converts historical 07:00–19:00 manual counts using factors learned *later*. An analyst comparing decades needs to know how sensitive such a backcast is to the vintage of the factor. A stable daily riding schedule predicts that a later factor reconstructs masked earlier days about as well as a contemporary factor; a change in when people cross predicts persistent error even for the same bridge and calendar month. [Nordback et al.](https://trec.pdx.edu/sites/default/files/recommend.pdf), “Estimating AADB” and the two twelve-hour scenarios, actually masked these hours at Colorado counters, but estimated an annual average rather than an earlier period's same-day totals. This is less ambitious and has an observable answer in the automatic-count era, not before it.

**Why start with the first?** The two bridge questions use public DOT counters but would inform different decisions: credit for new trips versus confidence in a measurement convention. The opening has a date, a physically plausible substitute route and a direct policy audience. I would switch to the measurement question if the bridge counts cannot distinguish the explanations even with both spans counted. [The historical counts catalogue](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/uczf-rk3c) identifies the series and its successor; it carries counter IDs and interval counts, not trip histories. No interval outcomes were queried for this shortlist.
