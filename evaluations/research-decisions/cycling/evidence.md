# Evidence for the Williamsburg Bridge count question

**Checked 25 November 2026.** Two self-checked passes: original results that could pre-empt or contradict either question, and documentation of what the counters measure. No bicycle count values were requested from the interval feed, no outcome comparison was computed, and no large dataset was downloaded.

## Prior results and contrary evidence

| Original source and passage inspected | Bearing on the decision |
|---|---|
| NYC DOT, [*Cyclist Counts at East River Bridge Locations: 24-Hour Weekday Counts, 1980 to 2025*](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/east-river-bridge-counts-24hrs-1980-2025.pdf), notes 1, 4 and 5; [methodology appendix](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/cycling-in-the-city-appendix.pdf), “Bike Counts.” | The oldest observations were single summer weekdays; later ones used multiple dates. The historical conversions drew on later automatic counts. Manual counts typically used dry nonholiday weekdays. The note gives a factor **range**, not its exact bridge/date assignments. |
| Nordback et al. (2013), [*Estimating Annual Average Daily Bicyclists*](https://doi.org/10.3141/2339-10), [authors' manuscript](https://trec.pdx.edu/sites/default/files/recommend.pdf), p. 5 (“The test data were not removed”), pp. 15–16, Tables 2–3. | Boulder already tested the identical 07:00–19:00 window, including a Tuesday–Thursday scenario. Short-count error depended on sampling duration. Their target was an **annual** average; the test records also contributed to factor construction. Here the target is the sum on the *actual masked dates*, and both calibration years are disjoint from both test years. |
| El Esawey et al. (2013), [*Development of Daily Adjustment Factors for Bicycle Traffic*](https://doi.org/10.1061/%28ASCE%29TE.1943-5436.0000565), [author's university record](https://scholar.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/179740), abstract, final three sentences. El Esawey (online 2022), [*Examination of the Temporal Stability of Daily and Monthly Adjustment Factors of Bicycle Traffic*](https://doi.org/10.1177/03611981221122784), [publisher-deposited abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/03611981221122784), sentences on dry summer weekdays. **Abstracts only.** | Vancouver already tested the backward-transfer idea: 2010 factors estimated 2009 monthly averages slightly better than 2011 factors. The longer study reports that dry summer weekday factors were unusually stable. These are plausible competing expectations, not independent replications: the city, researcher and parts of the periods overlap. Both concern **daily-to-monthly/yearly** scaling, not the fraction of one bridge day's traffic outside a 12-hour window. The Boulder records are independent. |
| Kuchkovska et al. (2025), [Brooklyn Bridge evaluation](https://doi.org/10.25140/2411-5363-2025-3%2841%29-310-321), pp. 310–321, indexed article text, Fig. 1, DiD section and Tables 2–3; [publisher-deposited abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.25140/2411-5363-2025-3%2841%29-310-321). [NYC DOT, Nov. 2021](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2021/brooklyn-bridge-bike-ridership-skyrockets.shtml), October comparison and following two paragraphs; [Dec. 2024](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2024/bicycle-ridership-all-time-high.shtml), four bridge-access bullets and following paragraph. | The study reports a positive relative change using Manhattan as the control. DOT describes nearby bridge growth as suggestive of new trips but also records concurrent investments. These are interpretations of related traffic counts, not independent trip histories. Counting a passage cannot reveal what its rider previously did. |
| MTA, [*Weeknight and Weekend Service*, job 19_071 M](https://www.mta.info/document/8071), “Weeknights 10 PM to 5 AM”; [L Project resources](https://www.mta.info/project/l-project/resources), “Project timing and news.” NYC DOT, [Dec. 2017 release](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2017/pr17-080.shtml), paragraph beginning “Even prior to the Canarsie Tunnel shutdown.” | The actual 2019 service plan reduced cross-river train frequency at night, with a ramp-down beginning at 20:00. The earlier DOT release describes changes to the bridge approaches. An altered night/day ratio could reflect several changes. The transit timetable motivates a **stress test** of a measurement rule, not a treatment assignment. |

The targeted search used variants of *Brooklyn Bridge lane diversion / induced trips / Manhattan Bridge control*, *12-hour bicycle count expansion / 7am–7pm*, *temporal stability / backward transfer of bicycle adjustment factors*, and *Williamsburg Bridge 12-hour conversion validation*. Following the Vancouver stability paper's references identified the earlier backward test; following DOT's ridership page led to the dated count table. These precedents narrow the contribution to a local test of calibration year and within-day timing, not a claim to have invented count expansion or temporal validation.

## Data and access

* **Counter identity.** In the [historical site record](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/smn3-rzf9.json?id=100009427), `100009427` is “Williamsburg Bridge Bike Path,” interval `15`, `US/Eastern;DST`, `sens=5` (no direction distinction in this export). [Site dictionary](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/smn3-rzf9/files/830f43cb-31fc-41fa-8db2-9ef117cd5bb8), *Column Information*, rows 10–14. The [successor hardware register](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/6up2-gnw8.json?id=100009427) lists serial `Y2H13074108`, installed 2013-12-03 and detached 2022-05-12, spanning the proposed comparison. This is stronger than matching on a bridge name; it does not rule out undetected faults. Do not add totals from the newer directional feed.
* **Passages.** Use [Bicycle Counts (Historical), `uczf-rk3c`](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/uczf-rk3c), not the similarly named sensors table. [Counts dictionary](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/uczf-rk3c/files/599ed6b1-9392-46ef-82e8-7383d3336e64), *Dataset Information* B16–B20 and *Column Information* rows 5–8: `id` is the site join, `date` is a local timestamp, `counts` measures passages, `status` identifies raw (0), excluded (1), deleted (2), modified (4), validated (8) and certified (16). The historical feed was replaced on 2026-01-28. To check that dates were addressable, six requests selected **only** `id,date,status` with `limit=1` for this ID, July 8–29 of each year 2014–19; each returned a timestamp. [Example, 2014](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/uczf-rk3c.json?%24select=id%2Cdate%2Cstatus&%24where=id%3D100009427%20AND%20date%3E%3D%272014-07-08T00%3A00%3A00%27%20AND%20date%3C%272014-07-30T00%3A00%3A00%27&%24limit=1). That checks access, not whole-day completeness.
* **Eligibility if the test proceeds.** Use 96 distinct quarter-hour bins on each summer day; never turn a missing bin into zero. Admit only nonmissing, nonnegative counts with status 0, 8 or 16; report raw versus reviewed coverage, omit modified days from the primary comparison. Confirm start/end-of-bin alignment or repeat with a one-bin shift. If results depend on that shift, a sensor outage, or eligibility concentrated in only a week or two of a month, stop. Require common month/weekday coverage across summers, multiple weeks per stratum, and report lost days by reason. The small status previews cannot establish all of this in advance.
* **Rain.** Use NOAA GHCN-Daily, [Central Park station `USW00094728`](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/cdo-web/datasets/GHCND/stations/GHCND:USW00094728/detail); join its date to the counter's local calendar date. [GHCN-Daily readme v3.35](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/pub/data/ghcn/daily/readme.txt), §III, `PRCP`, `MFLAG` and `QFLAG`; [two-day off-study schema check](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=daily-summaries&stations=USW00094728&startDate=2012-07-09&endDate=2012-07-10&dataTypes=PRCP&includeAttributes=true&format=json) returned `DATE`, `STATION`, `PRCP`, `PRCP_ATTRIBUTES` without a key. Use measured zero with blank quality and measurement flags; exclude trace, missing and presumed-zero reports. This conservative gauge rule approximates dry conditions; it cannot detect a shower confined to the bridge. Compare against all otherwise eligible days as a sensitivity check rather than assuming the gauge reproduces DOT's classifications.
* **Access and reuse.** NYC's [Open Data FAQ](https://www.nyc.gov/opendata/get-started/FAQs) permits use; its [Public Policies](https://cityofnewyork.github.io/opendatatsm/publicpolicies.html), “Explicit Identification” and “Terms of Use,” cover source/version/modifications, no accuracy warranty and possible revisions. NOAA's [GHCN-Daily record](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C00861/html), “Use Constraints” and “Fees,” and the readme specify dataset/subset, access date and methods citation. The proposed comparison needs filtered public requests and arithmetic, not a paid routing service, user tracks or a citywide export.
