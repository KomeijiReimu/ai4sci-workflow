# Can bin placement change a binary’s differential eclipse timing by 60 seconds?

**Recommendation.** Test the timing estimator separately on Kepler binaries [KIC 6836140 and KIC 10861842](03-evidence.md#data-that-can-decide-the-test). For each orbit, let **D = (primary timing residual − secondary timing residual)/2**, with each residual measured against its own predicted eclipse time. The question is whether bin placement contributes at least 60 seconds of **semi-amplitude in D** at the predicted sampling beat (3.7 and 4.9 days, respectively). This threshold makes no claim about either eclipse alone: signed primary and secondary coefficients of `(120, 0)` and `(60, −60)` seconds along the same phase axis give the same differential. Equal shifts cancel regardless of size.

**First test.** Use quarter 11, month 1 to calibrate; reserve month 2 for comparison. [Murphy, §3, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.6184) merged 30 consecutive short exposures on the flight grid, omitting incomplete bins; even then, processed spectra differed from native long cadence. Check that reconstruction against timings remeasured from the actual long-cadence flux. Then move the bin boundaries by 15 short exposures. Keep the integration duration, detrending and eligible eclipse pairs fixed; use timestamps rather than filling gaps. Compare the three-point minimum estimator with a profile fit using both eclipse slopes and accounting for exposure duration, particularly for flat-bottomed KIC 10861842.

Fit sine and cosine coefficients of D at the beat using the same flight-grid phase reference for both bin origins and the minute series. At the fundamental, a grid-locked component changes sign under the half-bin shift: the half-difference of coefficient vectors estimates the reversing component; the half-sum estimates what survives. Preserve shared noise by resampling contiguous blocks of orbits jointly and report 95% uncertainty regions. In month 1, check a stationary profile and recovery of an imposed 60-second differential sinusoid. Stop for a target if either month lacks three usable beat cycles, the slopes cannot locate the centers, the injection cannot be distinguished from zero, or reconstruction disagrees with native timings at the decision scale.

**Use of the result.** Flag a reversing component supported at or above 60 seconds for that target and estimator. Retain a surviving component supported at that scale and consistent in phase with the minute data for *photometric* follow-up. Report both if present, an upper bound on the relevant **differential beat amplitude** if warranted, or no decision if uncertainty crosses the threshold. Minute data are not independent proof of stellar motion: [Gilliland et al., §IV.1, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1001.0142) found instrumental harmonics even at short cadence. The test cannot identify spots or a third body; D largely subtracts a common light-travel delay. No eclipse timings have been measured here.
