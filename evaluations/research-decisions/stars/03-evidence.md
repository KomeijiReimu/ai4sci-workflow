# Evidence for the eclipse-timing decision

Checked 25 November 2026. The [initial choice](01-initial-choice.md) considered either attributing binary timing shifts to spots or adjudicating disputed stellar rotation periods. The [recommendation](02-recommendation.md) drops the spot-attribution claim and changes the comparison from two archive products to **two integration grids on identical short-cadence measurements**.

## Closest original results and challenges

| Source and precise locator | Evidence checked and consequence |
|---|---|
| Rappaport et al. (2013), [*Triple-Star Candidates Among the Kepler Binaries*](https://arxiv.org/html/1302.0563), §§3.1–3.2, Eqs. 4–5 | Used a three-point minimum estimator and eliminated candidate triples whose timing periods matched predicted cadence beats. They note annual variation of the beat from barycentric time corrections. A frequency coincidence is already a published veto, not a discovery to claim. |
| Tran et al. (2013), [*The Anticorrelated Nature of the Primary and Secondary Eclipse Timing Variations for the Kepler Contact Binaries*](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.4639v2), §§2.2, 3.1, 7; Fig. 7 | Their five-day smoothing suppresses rapid structure; they identify instrumental beat frequencies in the unsmoothed series and explain longer, often 50–200-day differential variations with spot migration. They already used sums and differences of eclipse times. Our contrast concerns only the predicted short-period beat component. |
| Balaji et al. (2015), [*Tracking the Stellar Longitudes of Starspots in Short-Period Kepler Binaries*](https://arxiv.org/html/1412.8101v2), §§2, 7, Appendix A | Tracked phase through 414 systems selected for anticorrelation. The authors distinguish asynchronous rotation, differential rotation, and migration of the locus of spot formation. An invariant timing component alone would distinguish none of these. This is a new analysis of overlapping Kepler observations, not independent observational confirmation of Tran. |
| Conroy et al. (2014), [*Kepler Eclipsing Binary Stars. IV*](https://arxiv.org/html/1306.0512v2), §§2.3–2.5; Figs. 4–5 | An out-of-eclipse flux imbalance pulls fitted primary and secondary times in opposite directions. Fitting the whole orbit can suppress this effect **and** real apsidal motion. The team measured available short-cadence timings, including a targeted short-period detached sample. Comparing two archives alone cannot establish a spot origin. |
| Kirk et al. (2016), [*Kepler Eclipsing Binary Stars. VII*](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.08830#S5), §5, Fig. 3 | Compared short-cadence curves against long-cadence deconvolution for two stars. Agreement after reconvolution need not imply the correct resolved profile. Use actual minute data rather than deconvolved half-hour data as the reference. |
| Marcadon & Prša (2024), [*Precision timing of eclipsing binaries from TESS full frame images*](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07694v2), §3.2, Table 2; §4.1, Fig. 3; §4.3, Table 4 | Independent **TESS observations**, not another reduction of Kepler: measured timings in two modes for five systems and recovered outer-orbit parameters in each. In two asymmetric systems the symmetry-based estimator shifted the eclipse times in opposite directions relative to the template. Tests the premise of the first draft and motivates a profile-based cross-check; the published comparison uses actual observing modes rather than moving a fixed-width integration grid across the same input fluxes. |
| Reinhold et al. (2023), [*New rotation period measurements of 67,163 Kepler stars*](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.04272), §§4.1, 4.5, Fig. 8, Table C.1; Lu et al. (2022), [*Meta-analysis of photometric and asteroseismic measurements of stellar rotation periods*](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05411v2), §§3.2, 6, Table 1, Fig. 19 | Reinhold report an autocorrelation pile-up near 15 days absent in the gradient-derived distribution, but also individual credible periods near 15 days. Their table distinguishes the two estimates, peak height, and choice of method by KIC. Lu compared seismic periods with several photometric estimators and tabulated the precision limit. This already covers generic seismic validation; the narrower disputed weak-peak subset has no verified usable overlap here. |

**Scope.** Targeted web and original-paper searches combined *Kepler contact binaries* with *opposed/anticorrelated minima, cadence beats, stroboscopic timing, rebinning, short vs long cadence* and *even/odd cadence phase*. A separate pass searched *15-day pile-up, half period, seismic splitting* and *Kepler rotation*. Followed Tran’s reference to Rappaport’s beat rejection; checked a cross-mission timing comparison as well as same-mission precedents. The cited original passages, not counts of search hits, support the decision. This is a local screen, not a systematic novelty claim. The literature and archive checks were self-checked in separate passes.

## Data that can decide the test

**Catalog metadata.** The Villanova [third revision and publication record](https://keplerebs.villanova.edu/papers) identifies Kirk et al. as the catalog reference. Its [field and file dictionary](https://keplerebs.villanova.edu/about#OnlineCatalog) defines `KIC`, `Period` (days), `BJD0` (BJD minus 2400000 at primary), eclipse depths/widths, separation in orbital phase, `Morph`, `In Catalog`, cadence links and flags. A secondary depth/width of −1 is a nondetection, not a usable eclipse; `FB` means flat-bottomed. The separate `etv` files contain derived timings, not a reference clock. No `etv` files were opened.

|---|---|---|

| [KIC 10861842](https://keplerebs.villanova.edu/overview/?k=10861842) | 0.4719432; 0.5055; 0.76. Positive depths; one ephemeris; `FB`. Useful stress case for the minimum estimator. | [MAST directory, 010861842](https://archive.stsci.edu/missions/kepler/lightcurves/0108/010861842/) |

**Exact products verified.** In each directory, the three filenames are `kplr<ID>-2011303113607_slc.fits`, `kplr<ID>-2011334093404_slc.fits` and `kplr<ID>-2012004120508_llc.fits`, where `<ID>` is the nine-digit ID above. Read header ranges (bytes 0–17279 per file) via the public [MAST Kepler mirror](https://registry.opendata.aws/mast-kepler/). All six have `DATA_REL=25`, `QUARTER=11`; the short-cadence files cover approximately BKJD 1001–1032 and 1033–1063, within the long-cadence interval 1001–1098. `NUM_FRM` is 9 versus 270 and `TIMEDEL` is 0.000681119940564 versus 0.02043359821692 days, respectively. These establish overlap and nominal spacing, not usable timing precision. The rough beat-duration estimates use only catalog period and spacing: orbital period divided by the distance of their ratio from the nearest integer. Use actual target timestamps for the proposed measurement.

**Rows, units and matching.** In the `LIGHTCURVE` extension, `TIME` is the barycentric midpoint in BKJD; add `BJDREFI+BJDREFF` (reference 2454833 here) for absolute BJD. Do not compare it directly to Villanova’s `BJD0` or add `TIMECORR` a second time. `CADENCENO` identifies samples *within* a cadence mode; join across modes on target, quarter and exposure windows, not equal cadence numbers. `SAP_FLUX` and `SAP_FLUX_ERR` are aperture flux and uncertainty in electrons/s; `SAP_QUALITY`, finite flux, and contiguous sample numbers identify usable intervals. `PDCSAP_FLUX` is a processed alternative, not an independent observation. See the [MAST Kepler Archive Manual](https://archive.stsci.edu/files/live/sites/mast/files/home/missions-and-data/k2/_documents/MAST_Kepler_Archive_Manual_2020.pdf), §2.3.1, Table 2-3 and App. A.1; [DR25 notes](https://archive.stsci.edu/kepler/release_notes/release_notes25/KSCI-19065-002DRN25.pdf), Table 2 and §§A.1.6–7, on Q11 cadence intervals and earlier short-cadence calibration errors. The [product guide](https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/Kepler_Data_Products_Overview.html), “Light Curve Files: Nominal Processing,” distinguishes the flight data from injection products. No archive flux or timing outcome rows were analysed.
