# Independent reading: two question-selection decisions

**Cycling:** do not use the bridge counters to attribute newly induced trips. The replacement question is worth a limited test: can two later calibrations recover the *recorded* totals on the same earlier Williamsburg Bridge days? **Stars:** do not use agreement between two Kepler products to attribute timing shifts to spots. The revised, within-exposure comparison can test the sensitivity of a *differential timing estimate* to bin placement. It does not by itself show that both eclipses move in opposite directions.

### Cycling: bridge opening → count reconstruction

**1 · Prior result.** The initial proposal correctly says that [Kuchkovska, Lysetska & Fundak, DiD section and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.25140/2411-5363-2025-3%2841%29-310-321) use Manhattan as the control. More of the simple comparison is already available than the shortlist suggests. In the published additive model, holding the other covariates fixed, the common post coefficient is +17,950 passages/month and the Brooklyn × post coefficient is +26,713. Thus the *fitted* two-bridge sum changes by `2 × 17,950 + 26,713 = 62,613` per month. This is arithmetic on published coefficients, not a new count, a causal estimate of induced trips, or a comparison to a separate background series. [DOT’s November 2021 release](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2021/brooklyn-bridge-bike-ridership-skyrockets.shtml), immediately after its October figures, had already compared the other bridges with historical trends.

For the final proposal, [DOT’s historical table, notes 1, 4–5](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/east-river-bridge-counts-24hrs-1980-2025.pdf) says 07:00–19:00 counts were expanded by 1.25–1.59 using three years of later automated counts. [Nordback et al., study-data paragraph and Tables 2–3](https://trec.pdx.edu/sites/default/files/recommend.pdf) test the same 12-hour weekday window, but for annual average daily bicyclists; test observations contributed to the factors. El Esawey’s [Vancouver study, abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/03611981221122784) finds especially stable *daily-to-longer-period* factors for dry summer weekdays. A closer counterexample for the **target** is [Roll, §3.5.1, Fig. 3.12 and Table 3.17](https://pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/open_access_etds/998/): summer-derived two-hour peak factors were tested against **observed 24-hour totals** on independent fall counts; the sums from local AM and PM factors overshot by about 6% and 17%. Held-out validation of a whole day is not new. Different sites, a different season and a two-hour window do not settle the two-vintage, one-bridge, same-earlier-days comparison. The narrowed overlap claim stands; Roll’s percentages are not an error forecast for New York.

**2 · Data.** The initial two bridge series exist, but the [count dictionary, “Column Information,” rows 5–8](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/uczf-rk3c/files/599ed6b1-9392-46ef-82e8-7383d3336e64) identifies counter, timestamp, passages and record status—not prior route, prior mode or an unaffected comparison population. For the final test the missing hours are in the same counter series. I independently opened the [site row](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/smn3-rzf9.json?id=100009427), [site dictionary, rows 10–14](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/api/views/smn3-rzf9/files/830f43cb-31fc-41fa-8db2-9ef117cd5bb8) and [hardware register](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/6up2-gnw8.json?id=100009427): `100009427`, 15 minutes, Eastern with DST, historical export without direction distinction, serial `Y2H13074108` from 2013-12-03 to 2022-05-12. Do not combine historical totals with successor directional channels.

For each of 2014, 2015, 2018 and 2019 an administrative query to [the historical feed](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/uczf-rk3c.json) restricted the ID and dates to July 8 inclusive–September 1 exclusive and grouped by `status`, selecting `count(*)`, `count(distinct date)`, `min(date)`, `max(date)` only. Each returned **5,280 rows / 5,280 timestamps**, July 8 00:00 through August 31 23:45, all raw status 0; `55 × 96 = 5,280`. No passage value or distribution was requested. A [NOAA off-study request](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=daily-summaries&stations=USW00094728&startDate=2012-07-09&endDate=2012-07-10&dataTypes=PRCP&includeAttributes=true&format=json) returned date, station, precipitation and its flags. Calendar alone allows **24, 23, 24, 24** Tue–Thu dates respectively; each month × weekday cell has only **3–5** dates *before* rain and value-quality exclusions. Do not promise a precise week-resampled interval or use an empty resampled cell as zero. Bin start/end convention and the number of complete, eligible dry days remain open.

**3 · Rival explanations.** For the initial proposal, a switch of one crossing from Manhattan to Brooklyn adds two to the treated-minus-control contrast and zero to their sum. A switch from a third route and a genuinely new trip both add one to the sum. Even a stable sum can hide additions offset by other losses. This non-identification is decisive for the proposed policy attribution.

For the final proposal, write a stratum’s expansion as `1 + omitted passages / daytime passages`. On the same positive earlier daytime count a larger 2019 factor yields a **larger estimate**, not necessarily an **overestimate**: both later factors can be too small. A larger ratio also need not mean more night passages; the denominator can fall. Compare each vintage to the revealed total *and* compare their signed errors to one another. The [MTA service plan, p. 2, “Weeknights 10 PM to 5 AM”](https://www.mta.info/document/8071) confirms a ramp-down at 20:00 and 20-minute cross-river service from 22:00. Split local 20:00–24:00 plus 00:00–05:00 from the other omitted bins; neither that timing nor year labels identify who changed modes. [DOT’s May 2019 Grand Street update, “Design Justification” and “Remaining Work”](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/grand-st-update-may2019.pdf), documents work on a bridge connection between calibration summers. The test distinguishes **calibration errors in recorded traffic**, not their unique behavioral cause.

A site-specific check cuts both ways. [Boeri, §7 “Comparing Results of NYC DOT Automated Bicycle Counts with Manual Counts,” pp. 56–58, Table 12; limits p. 62](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/74c026e7-5fce-4455-b885-9f8aeb7e7e2d/content) found that the Williamsburg manual count was **9.11% higher** for *all* vehicles, but **0.23% lower** for bikes and e-bikes alone. The camera and loop were on opposite sides of the bridge. This was later daytime fieldwork, on a different hardware vintage. It neither shows that the proposed counter saturated in 2014–19 nor validates its historical night counts to 5%. It supports retaining “recorded passages” as the unit.

**4 · Decision and next action.** The initial shortlist explicitly favored estimating credit for the protected lane over studying a measurement convention, then named the condition that would reverse the choice. The final proposal meets that condition and identifies a decision a historian could actually make: retain a simple conversion for comparable summer averages, show vintage sensitivity, or report insufficient precision. **Keep the infrastructure no-go. Advance only the recorded-count test to design:** specify the paired vintage contrast, timestamp convention and minimum dry coverage for all six cells before unmasking. The ±5% applies to error in levels, not to equivalence of the two factors or to a DOT standard. The official table describes factors based on data starting in 2014; testing 2018/19 on 2014/15 does not reproduce the official historical factors or verify an unobserved night in 1980.

### Stars: two observing modes → two bin origins on the same exposures

**1 · Prior result.** [Tran et al., §2.2, Table 2 and §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.4639) report anticorrelated eclipse timings and caution about cadence beats: the five-day smoothing suppresses rapid signals. [Conroy et al., §§2.4–2.5, Figs. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.0512) did more than warn about sparse short cadence: they measured short-cadence timings where available, requested data on 31 apparently detached short-period binaries, and demonstrated fictitious antiphase shifts from profile imbalance. [Marcadon & Prša, §§2.1, 3.2, 4.1, 4.3, Fig. 3 and Tables 2, 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07694v2) measured both modes for five TESS systems and found opposite estimator-dependent shifts in two asymmetric light curves. This is independent observing evidence, not the same Kepler stars or a test of spot migration at the proposed beat. The *generic* initial comparison was already performed; its intended spot attribution was not answered by it.

For the final test an even closer procedural precedent is [Murphy, §3, Fig. 3 and following text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.6184): groups of 30 short-cadence points on the **actual** long-cadence grid were merged and compared with native long cadence, rejecting incomplete groups; a near-constant star’s processed spectra still differed. [Rappaport et al., §3.2, Eqs. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.0563), already predict the sampling beat and describe excluding matching periods in a triple-star search. Neither passage changes the origin by half a bin on the same eclipses to estimate a held-out differential amplitude. The sign change of a grid-locked fundamental under a half-cycle shift is arithmetic, not a prospective discovery. Its size, reproducibility and coexistence with a resolved displacement for these two systems remain empirical questions.

**2 · Data.** The initial proposal names catalogs and notes that overlap was unknown. A short-cadence sample deliberately recruited for *detached* binaries is not automatically the contact-binary population whose anticorrelation motivated the question, nor does a one-month series settle a 50–200-day modulation. The final proposal changes its target rather than treating it as a matched replication. I opened the Villanova rows for [KIC 6836140](https://keplerebs.villanova.edu/overview/?k=6836140) and [KIC 10861842](https://keplerebs.villanova.edu/overview/?k=10861842): periods 0.4877210 and 0.4719432 d, secondary phases 0.4972 and 0.5055, two nonzero eclipse depths each; `FB` on the second. A catalog width or depth is not timing precision.

I requested only FITS bytes 0–17279 for each of the six [MAST mirror](https://registry.opendata.aws/mast-kepler/) objects. The exact key is `kepler/public/lightcurves/<first4>/<id>/kplr<id>-<suffix>.fits` in `stpubdata.s3.amazonaws.com`: IDs `006836140`, `010861842`; suffixes `2011303113607_slc`, `2011334093404_slc`, `2012004120508_llc` ([one header source](https://stpubdata.s3.amazonaws.com/kepler/public/lightcurves/0068/006836140/kplr006836140-2011303113607_slc.fits)). All returned HTTP **206**. Per star the files are 4,593,600, 4,464,000 and 506,880 bytes: **19,128,960 bytes together**, consistent with “less than 20 MB.” Q11, DR25, `NUM_FRM` 9 versus 270, `TIMEDEL` 0.000681119940564 versus 0.02043359821692 d; short intervals ~1001.2–1032.3 and 1033.0–1063.2 lie inside ~1001.2–1098.3 (TDB days relative to BJD 2454833). `TIMEPIXR=0.5`, `CADENCENO`, `SAP_FLUX`, `SAP_FLUX_ERR` and `SAP_QUALITY` support midpoint/window matching, not an across-mode join on equal cadence numbers. No light-curve or catalog timing rows were read. Three cycles fit nominally in either month; usable eclipse pairs and one-minute sensitivity require the proposed gates.

**3 · Rival explanations.** A resolved feature that remains in the original minute data counts against the claim that **coarse sampling alone** creates it. It does not distinguish spots from evolving asymmetry, blends or a fitting bias shared across products. In the final proposal the same photons, eclipse pairs and integration duration are held fixed; the two coarse estimates are correlated by construction. A component that reverses with the bin shift is grid-sensitive for the chosen estimator. The held-out month, stationary control, opposite-shift injection, resolved slopes and joint noise treatment are material improvements over the initial comparison.

The **differential** is not a measurement of two opposite amplitudes. With `D=(primary−secondary)/2`, coefficient pairs `(120 s, 0 s)` and `(60 s, −60 s)` have the same 60-s differential along a given phase axis. Nor do the two minima naturally fall half a coarse bin apart. Using catalog period `P`, cadence `C` and secondary phase `s`, the nominal beat `P/abs(P/C−round(P/C))` is **3.711 and 4.894 d**, as proposed, but `sP/C` is **11.867 and 11.675 bins**, respectively. These are positions on the **sampling grid**, not the roughly half-orbit separation. The two individual distortions can partly cancel in `D`. A reversing `D` still establishes sensitivity of `D`; a small `D` is not an upper bound on *each eclipse’s* sampling error. An unchanged component in shared data is not an independent confirmation of an astrophysical origin. [Gilliland et al., §IV.1, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0142), even detected instrument-related long-cadence harmonics in short-cadence data in Q1; that is a counterexample to treating minute data as an infallible clock, not evidence of contamination in these Q11 targets.

**4 · Decision and next action.** The initial author preferred contemporaneous measurements to a weak-rotation-peak/seismic comparison with unknown overlap. [Lu et al., §3.2 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05411), place only 33 of 70 stars without reported companions in their group with relative seismic uncertainty ≤0.4; this is before selecting a disputed photometric period. That supports the opportunity-cost argument, not a claim that the rotation route is impossible. **Advance the revised two-star diagnostic to design, not the spot test.** Keep 60 s as a chosen *differential* follow-up threshold and retain the calibration stop rules. Either narrow the opening question to `D`, or require both primary and secondary coefficient vectors and their joint uncertainty before claiming “one early, the other late.” Retain a surviving feature for photometric follow-up; flag a reversing one for this target and estimator; leave ambiguous cases undecided. This contrast largely subtracts a common light-travel delay and cannot reverse Rappaport’s triple-candidate decisions or establish a population rate.
