# Open questions from two broad directions

This product check examines the question-selection instructions in the working checkout. The operator receives one broad domain and the idea skill, not a research question, preferred paper, dataset, contrast or expected answer. The two domains were chosen before either operator proposed a question. This is a test of the normal research workflow with access to literature, not a closed-book selection or an assessment of competing products.

| Case | Research brief |
|---|---|
| Urban cycling | Choose a computational research question about urban bicycle travel that is worth investigating with public evidence. Recommend a first test. Do not run the experiment. |
| Variable stars | Choose a computational research question about observed stellar variability that is worth investigating with public evidence. Recommend a first test. Do not run the experiment. |

Each investigator works on one domain. Preserve their initial choice or no-go, decisive sources and feasibility check, substantive objection, and any revision. A new reader examines both only after that first work is saved. The reader may search for an earlier answer the author missed. Record whether objections change the recommendation. Do not replace an unproductive case with a new topic.

For each case the reader checks four things:

1. Does the closest accessible prior result actually support the stated overlap or disagreement? Identify its passage, figure or table. An older result that answers the proposed comparison can defeat it.
2. Do the stated data, including the needed units, identifiers, resolution and comparison groups, exist and permit the proposed test? Metadata is not a measured result.
3. Under the same feasible comparison, what observations would distinguish the rival explanations? What remains ambiguous?
4. What scientific interpretation or practical decision changes under either outcome, why did this question beat its strongest alternative, and where did the question originate?

Report each check as supported, partial or unsupported with its evidence. A defensible no-go is useful but is not a viable new question. If a reader reopens a flaw, preserve the original and evaluate a bounded revision separately. These are two opportunities for an agent to make a choice, not proof of general novelty or superiority over a leaderboard configuration.
